## Patterns (inference, not stated by judges)
1. Tactile, satisfying, replayable interaction shows up in most first places ("designed to feel tactile, clean, and satisfying" is how #10 describes Axis).
2. Real-world utility and teaching Lenses place when the problem is instantly recognisable (DIY, language, wildlife safety, emergency skills such as an EpiPen training Lens reported earlier).
3. Many winners use a distinct theme and a clear first ten seconds.
4. Features that need AI, cloud profiles, voice or cross-session memory (Decisionator, Remember) cannot be shown in this hackathon's preview; the plan-lens references say to stand in or avoid them.

## What this means for EarthProof
- The rover ramp trial is a toy: no recognisable real problem, no emotional stake. Rejected.
- Home Check keeps what is strong (deterministic, tested, evidence pointing at the failing part, real-size placement) and aims it at a problem everyone recognises: will this door and this ramp work for someone I care about.
- It adds tactile direct manipulation (drag the door frame, drag the ramp foot) because tactile interaction is what most winners share.
- It does not claim to beat the winners above; this hackathon (Learn AR with SPECS, 1188 participants on 2026-11-08) is judged on Design, Technical Implementation, Innovation/Idea and Presentation, and the gallery was unpublished, so no comparison with live entries is possible.
